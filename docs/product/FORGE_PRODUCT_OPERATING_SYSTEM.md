# FORGE Product Operating System

**Status:** Foundational Draft
**Abbreviation:** FPOS

## Purpose

FPOS defines how FORGE products are conceived, researched, evaluated, designed, prioritized, implemented, measured, and evolved.

## Product Lifecycle

1. Vision alignment
2. Market research
3. Market-leader analysis
4. Customer-problem analysis
5. User-complaint analysis
6. Competitive comparison
7. Innovation opportunity analysis
8. Product architecture
9. UX and workflow design
10. Repository gap analysis
11. Engineering architecture
12. Implementation
13. Validation
14. Release
15. Outcome measurement
16. Product learning

- Approved
- Planned
- In Development
- Beta

- Declined

## Feature Investment Analysis

Every significant feature should evaluate:

- demand;
- affected users;
- expected customer value;
- implementation effort;
- one-time cost;
- recurring infrastructure cost;
- AI usage cost;
- third-party fees;
- compliance impact;
- support impact;
- technical-debt risk;
- architectural reuse;
- revenue or retention potential;
- expected payback;
- long-term maintenance burden.

## Product Decision Rule

Features are not approved solely because:

- a competitor has them;
- users voted for them;
- they are technically interesting;
- they are fashionable.

They are approved when evidence, strategic fit, cost, architecture, and customer value support investment.

## Vision Traceability

Every initiative should identify:

- the North-Star outcome it supports;
- the product area that owns it;
- shared platform capabilities it uses;
- governing product decisions;
- governing engineering architecture;
- repository implementation;
- validation evidence.

## Product and Engineering Relationship

Product Engineering determines what should be built, for whom, and why.

Software Engineering determines how approved capabilities should be built safely and correctly.
