# FORGE Product Constitution

**Status:** Foundational Draft

## 1. Learn from Market Leaders

Study the products with the strongest adoption and understand why their workflows succeed.

## 2. Improve, Do Not Blindly Imitate

Adopt proven product patterns where they work. Redesign them where customers experience friction.

## 3. Users Are the Final Authority

Competitor behavior is evidence. Customer outcomes determine value.

## 4. Innovation Must Earn Its Place

A unique capability should:

- solve a real problem;
- improve speed, confidence, revenue, savings, or accuracy;
- reuse existing architecture where practical;
- avoid disproportionate technical or financial debt;
- remain understandable to ordinary users.

## 5. Product Before Technology

Engineering enables customer outcomes. Architecture is not itself the product.

## 6. Invisible Complexity

Complex processing should produce simple, clear, explainable outcomes.

## 7. Every Feature Is an Investment

Feature decisions must consider:

- customer demand;
- customer value;
- implementation effort;
- recurring operating cost;
- third-party cost;
- support burden;
- technical-debt risk;
- strategic reuse;
- expected return.

## 8. Cost Transparency

Public roadmaps may disclose understandable cost ranges and tradeoffs.

Sensitive internal financial details remain internal.

## 9. Opportunity Over Advertising

FORGE should not force users to view advertisements.

The Opportunity Center should show optional:

- rebates;
- discounts;
- incentives;
- trusted providers;
- product comparisons;
- total-cost-of-ownership comparisons.

Recommendations must remain independent of sponsorship.

## 10. Recommend Ownership Value

FORGE should evaluate total cost, expected life, operating cost, warranty, repair exposure, and customer circumstances—not sticker price alone.

## 11. Earn Expansion

Each new customer relationship must be earned by delivering value in the current relationship.

## 12. Community-Driven Evolution

Users may submit, discuss, follow, and vote on ideas.

Votes are evidence, not automatic authority.

## 13. Explain Decisions

Planned, deferred, declined, and released ideas should have understandable reasons.

## 14. Preserve Strategic Ideas

No strategic idea should remain only in chat, memory, or informal notes.

## 15. Protect Trust

Recommendations must disclose:

- supporting evidence;
- assumptions;
- uncertainty;
- confidence;
- sponsorship or commercial relationships.

## 16. Every Participant Receives Value

Landlords, contractors, manufacturers, service providers, and other participants should receive clear value from joining the ecosystem.
